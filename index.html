utkarsh singh

I am a visual artist working across photography, video, text, performance, and books. My work blends real stories with fiction to explore the human condition within broader historical and regional contexts, with a focus on cultural anthropology, migration, decoloniality, and dissent.

Line Across the Sky

The project seeks to highlight the constructed and often arbitrary nature of nationality, borders, and citizenship, focusing on long histories of migration and cultural anthropology. We'll Go Back to Our Roots When We Reach the Sky

This book explores the journey of individuals from the global south migrating to germany. Their story dives into the complexities of seeking home in a foreign land, sparking reflections on hope, loss and memory. The very act of leaving is a transformative pilgrimage, a metamorphosis that extends far beyond the mere act of arrival.

Till We All Breathe the Same

The story presents a dystopian world transitioning from amnesia to the act of dissent. The exploration of themes such as the nature of reality, the manipulation of individuals within systems, and the struggle for freedom resonates with many existential and philosophical inquiries. The character's progression from hedonism to a deeper understanding of the world around him highlights the complexities of human motivation and desire.

In a world where every unconventional act is inherently political, the will to call for action encapsulates the moral and ethical dilemmas inherent in societal change and resistance against oppressive systems. It ponders the significance of their own choices in shaping the course of history, emphasizing the need to address segregation and misinformation as catalysts for hatred.

Can You Smell the Divine Fragrance in Town

The project documents the rapid transformation of land between Gandhinagar and Ahmedabad, beginning with construction sites, the workers and their families who sustain them and gradually expanding to landscapes overwhelmed by human presence. Situated within the context of the Anthropocene, the work studies the changing relationship between humans, land, and regulation, where growth steadily replaces memory, nature, and rural life, all in the name of development.
